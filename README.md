# The Photoelectric Effect — Virtual Physics Laboratory

An interactive, high-precision educational virtual physics laboratory designed for **B.Tech 1st-Year Engineering Physics** students and faculty.

## 🔬 Project Overview
This laboratory simulation demonstrates the microscopic quantum mechanics of the Photoelectric Effect, originally explained by Albert Einstein in 1905 (earning the 1921 Nobel Prize in Physics). It allows students to explore how light frequency, photon energy, light intensity, metal work function, and retarding/accelerating tube voltages affect electron emission and stopping potential.

## ✨ Key Features
- **Dynamic Apparatus Simulation:** Real-time canvas rendering of a quartz vacuum phototube, incident photons, and liberated photoelectrons.
- **Synchronized Collision Audio:** Distinct, subtle particle detector audio clicks strictly when electrons strike the collector anode plate via Web Audio API.
- **Interactive Laboratory Record Book:** Tabulate experimental trials under varying parameters. Clicking any logged observation dynamically plots its exact physical parameters on the scientific graphs.
- **4 Live Scientific Graphs:**
  - $K_{\text{max}}$ vs. Frequency ($f$) — Slope gives Planck's constant $h$, X-intercept gives threshold frequency $f_0$.
  - Stopping Potential ($V_s$) vs. Frequency ($f$) — Slope gives $h/e$.
  - Photocurrent ($I$) vs. Intensity — Verifies linear proportionality for $f \ge f_0$.
  - $I-V$ Characteristic Curves — Demonstrates saturation current and negative stopping voltage.
- **Interactive Laboratory Challenges:** 4 guided practical tasks with automated validation and instant feedback.
- **Viva Voce Examination:** 10 comprehensive viva questions with scoring, instant feedback, and physical explanations.
- **CSV Data Export:** Export recorded laboratory trials to `.csv` format for external spreadsheet analysis.
